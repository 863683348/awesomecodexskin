Two hours of night coding leaves my eyes drier than a full workday in the office. The screen is the same. The difference is your body: after dark, your pupils are wider, your brain reads the bright rectangle as "morning", and your blink rate drops by a third. Picking the right Codex skin is one of the cheapest fixes for all three.

This is not about going fully dark "because dark themes are better". It's about three numbers: **luminance, saturation, and blue-light ratio**. Set those right and your eyes stop negotiating with the screen.

## The three numbers that actually matter

- **Background luminance**: pure black (#000000) is worse than most people think — it forces maximum contrast against everything else on screen and causes halation around bright text. A very dark gray (#111–#1A) reads more comfortably.
- **Saturation**: vivid accent colors are fine in small doses, but a whole UI full of saturated blues and reds makes the eye hunt for focus. Night skins should be mostly desaturated with one or two restrained accents.
- **Blue-light ratio**: warm-toned dark themes (amber, orange-brown, soft yellow) signal "evening" to your circadian system far more gently than a cool blue theme does.

A skin is a filter, not just decoration. If it fails these three, no amount of external blue-light glasses fully compensates.

## The 7 settings checklist

Work through these once, and you're done for the year:

1. **Switch to a dark skin, but not pure black.** Look for backgrounds around `#111`–`#1A1A1A`, not `#000000`. In this index, [Gothic Void Expedition](/skins/gothic-void-expedition/) is a good baseline — its built-in default uses a near-black that keeps halation low.
2. **Cap the accent saturation.** One or two accents max. [Cyber Neon](/skins/cyber-neon/) is the tempting extreme; save it for daytime, not midnight.
3. **Prefer warm neutrals after 9 PM.** [Amber Nocturne](/skins/amber-nocturne/) exists for exactly this — an amber-brown base that stays readable without shouting "blue light" at your brain.
4. **Lower Codex UI brightness if your engine supports it.** Some engines scale the whole palette; a 10–15% reduction is usually the sweet spot.
5. **Match your editor to your terminal.** A mismatched palette forces your eyes to re-adapt every time you switch panes. Same hue family, close luminance, done.
6. **Enable OS night shift / f.lux on top of the skin.** The skin handles UI colors; the OS layer warms the entire display. They're complementary, not alternatives.
7. **Check your blink rate — seriously.** A `20-20-20` reminder (every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away, 20 seconds) does more than any theme. Skin quality and blink breaks are the two halves of the fix.

## Why moody dark skins pass the test

[Mythic Guardian Noir](/skins/mythic-guardian-noir/) and the other dark-midnight entries in this index share one trait: they keep luminance low and consistent across panels, so your pupil doesn't constantly re-adjust. That consistency — not "how cool the theme looks" — is what makes a skin night-safe.

A good test: dim the room lights, open a skin, and squint. If the sidebar and the code area feel like one surface, the skin is doing its job. If the sidebar glows like a sign, move on.

## Quick FAQ

**Is a light theme at night always worse?**
Not always, but usually. The issue is the massive bright surface area, not the theme's "temperature". A dimmed, low-contrast light theme is fine; a full-brightness white theme at 2 AM is the hardest on your eyes.

**Do I need blue-light blocking glasses if my skin is warm?**
The skin is a start, not a cure. Warm themes reduce the blue-light dose; glasses and OS night shift reduce it further. Stack them if you code late every night.

**Can I just copy one of these skins and tweak it?**
Yes — that's the fastest path. Take [Amber Nocturne](/skins/amber-nocturne/) or [Gothic Void Expedition](/skins/gothic-void-expedition/), reduce saturation, and save it as your own "night mode" preset.
