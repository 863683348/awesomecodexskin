The light-vs-dark debate is the oldest argument in developer aesthetics, and the answer is the same as most aesthetic arguments: it depends. But "it depends" is useless without criteria. Here are the real factors, and which Codex skins in this index fit each scenario.

## The actual science, briefly

- **Dark themes** reduce screen brightness, which helps in low-light rooms and for people sensitive to bright surfaces. The catch: in a bright room, a dark screen reflects more glare and can make text harder to read.
- **Light themes** reflect ambient light instead of fighting it, so they often read better in daylight. The catch: a bright white surface in a dark room is hard on the eyes.
- **Pure black is not your friend** in either mode — `#000` backgrounds create halation; pure white text on pure black is genuinely worse for readability than a dark-gray pair.

The rule of thumb: **match your theme's luminance to your room's luminance.** Bright room → light theme. Dark room → dark theme. It's the same reason phones have automatic light/dark modes.

## Dark Codex skins: best for

- **Deep focus sessions at night.** A dark theme like [Gothic Void Expedition](/skins/gothic-void-expedition/) keeps the screen from dominating your peripheral vision.
- **Low-light offices.** Same reasoning.
- **Blue-light sensitivity.** Warm dark themes — [Amber Nocturne](/skins/amber-nocturne/) is a good example — are gentler on evening eyes than cool ones.

**Watch out for:** dark themes in bright rooms. If you're by a window, a dark screen turns into a mirror.

## Light Codex skins: best for

- **Daytime, near windows.** [Berry (light journal)](/skins/berry-light-journal/) or [Clear Glass](/skins/clear-glass/) read cleanly against daylight.
- **Screenshots and demos.** Light themes photograph better and feel more "default" to viewers.
- **People with astigmatism.** Many report less eye fatigue with light themes because text edges are sharper against a bright background.

**Watch out for:** light themes in dark rooms. Full-brightness white at 2 AM is the hardest thing on your eyes short of staring at a lamp.

## The hybrid setup

You don't have to pick. The best setup for most people:

- **Day default:** a light theme like [Clear Glass](/skins/clear-glass/).
- **Night default:** a dark theme like [Gothic Void Expedition](/skins/gothic-void-expedition/).
- **Switch** when the room changes — or automate it with your OS's night mode if your engine supports theme-on-time.

This is what your phone does every day. Your editor can too.

## Quick FAQ

**Is dark mode better for battery life?**
On OLED screens, mildly — dark pixels use less power. On LCD (most desktop monitors), essentially no difference.

**Are dark themes better for coding in general?**
No scientific consensus. It's preference plus room lighting. The "dark = pro" belief is cultural, not physiological.

**Can I use a light skin but keep dark panels?**
You can, but mixed luminance is the worst of both worlds — your eyes re-adapt constantly. Pick one side, or a theme designed as a cohesive light system like [Berry (light)](/skins/berry-light-journal/).

**What's the single best pick if I can only have one?**
A balanced dark theme — [Gothic Void Expedition](/skins/gothic-void-expedition/) — because dark works in most rooms, and you can pair it with OS night-mode brightness. If you're mostly in daylight, make it [Clear Glass](/skins/clear-glass/) instead.

Match your theme to your room, not to a trend. Light for daylight and demos, dark for nights and focus, and the hybrid setup if you want both.
