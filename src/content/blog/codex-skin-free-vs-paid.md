Let me save you some money: the best Codex skin you can install right now is almost certainly free. Of the 99 skins in this index, the overwhelming majority are open-source or free to use. Paid skins exist, and they're not a scam — but you should know exactly what you're paying for before you open your wallet.

## What free Codex skins give you

Free skins come from three places: open-source repos (like the Wangnov collection, the biggest single source in this index), engine authors shipping their own presets, and community galleries. What they all share:

- The complete theme — colors, prompts, or theme files, fully usable.
- Community maintenance — bugs get fixed by whoever cares.
- Zero risk — if you don't like it, you uninstall and you're out nothing.

A free skin like [Sakura Pink Dawn](/skins/sakura-pink-dawn/) or [Hatsune Miku](/skins/hatsune-miku/) is a complete, polished product. There is nothing "trial" about it.

## What paid Codex skins actually add

Paid skins (usually a few dollars) typically bundle four things free skins don't guarantee:

1. **Guaranteed updates.** A commercial skin promises compatibility with engine updates; a free one depends on community goodwill.
2. **Support.** An email or Discord where the author answers your setup questions.
3. **Exclusivity.** The palette isn't in every gallery, so your workspace looks less like a template.
4. **Consistent multi-tool matching.** Many paid packs ship the same palette for Codex, your terminal, and your editor — the sync work is done for you.

That last one is the only feature I'd genuinely pay for: a professionally matched palette across every surface you code on. [Berry (light journal)](/skins/berry-light/) in this index shows how much work a well-matched light palette is — doing it across four tools by hand is real labor.

## When free is enough

- You want one good skin for yourself.
- You're fine fixing or swapping a skin when an engine updates.
- You like browsing galleries and trying new looks monthly.

Free is not a compromise here. The open-source ecosystem for Codex skins is young but healthy, and the community repos are active.

## When paid is worth it

- You reskin a whole team and need consistency + support.
- You want a matched Codex+terminal+editor pack and don't want to DIY it.
- You use a niche engine where community skins are scarce — a commercial theme might be your only polished option.

## How to check a skin's price before installing

Every skin detail page in this index shows its source and author. Open-source repos are free by definition. For paid skins, check the source site before installing — and skip anything that demands payment for a basic preset. That's a pattern worth avoiding.

## Quick FAQ

**Are paid Codex skins better quality?**
Not automatically. Quality in skins is about palette discipline and maintenance, which free open-source projects often have plenty of. Paid buys you guarantees, not inherently better colors.

**Can I get a refund if a paid skin doesn't work?**
Depends on the store — most commercial theme shops offer 14-day refunds. Check before buying.

**Do free skins work with paid engines?**
Usually yes. A skin is content; an engine is the software that applies it. Free skins apply fine in paid engines like Dream Skin's pro tier, unless the engine artificially locks imports.
